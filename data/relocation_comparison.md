# Moved into the flood: Lamontville compared with 21 other cases

**The question:** eThekwini moved families displaced by the April 2022 floods into a "temporary" camp on the Umlazi riverbank at Lamontville. The camp flooded in February 2025 and 5 people died (2 women and 3 children). Where else have governments relocated displaced people onto flood-prone land, and what is common and different?

**Data:**
- **Sources:** 24 cases, each with its source URL, from `raw/relocation_into_floodzones_africa.json` (10 cases) and `raw/relocation_into_floodzones_world.json` (14 cases).
- **Counts:** 22 cases are counted. We left out one audit that isn't a relocation (ZA-KZN-04) and one relocation that is only alleged and hasn't happened (ZA-GP-01).
- **Outputs:** the table is `relocation_cases.csv`, built by `methods/scripts/build_relocation_comparison.ps1`. The codes are our reading of each case's evidence fields.
- **Confidence:** 10 high, 9 medium, 5 low.

## What is common (Lamontville is typical)
1. **The risk was on record, and the move went ahead.** In 12 of 22 cases, the risk was documented before people were moved or before the deadly flood.
   - **Durban:** the camp flooded in April 2022, the Mail & Guardian called it flood-prone that May, and the Auditor-General flagged unsuitable siting that August. The fatal flood came 2.5 years later.
   - **Haiti:** the UN migration agency (IOM) warned in writing in July 2010 that Corail floods "regularly at least once a year", and building went on.
   - **Philippines:** after the 2009 floods, the national housing agency built new units on the same flooded ground.
   - **Houston:** federal flood maps (1985) came before the federal housing subsidy (1991).
2. **"Temporary" becomes years.** 13 sites were announced as temporary, and in 11 people stayed more than a year: Durban 3+ years, Isipingo 13+, Bentiu about 10, and Haiti became a permanent city. The "temporary" label also justifies flimsy shelters: in Cox's Bazar, policy requires tarps.
3. **The same households are displaced again.**
   - **Durban:** Mega Village, then tents, then Gwala Street, then Lamontville, all on the same Umlaas/Umlazi river system.
   - **Elsewhere:** Semmencheri, Kasiglahan and Sindh follow the same cycle.
4. **No official accepted responsibility for the site: 0 of 22.**
   - **Blaming another body:** 7 cases.
   - **Blaming the weather:** 4 cases. In Durban the mayor cited "unprecedented rainfall".
   - **Blaming residents:** 2 cases. In Durban it was "dumping"; in Indonesia, "garbage-clogged drains".
   - **No official statement:** 9 cases.
5. **Almost no one is held to account.** 21 of 22 cases had no inquiry, sanction or compensation. The only legal action is a US tenants' lawsuit against the federal housing department (Arbor Court).
6. **No source confirms a warning reached the relocated people: 0 of 22.**
   - **Explicitly not warned:** Chennai (water came at about 3 am) and the Philippines (a dam released water without notice).
   - **Durban:** only a regional Yellow Level 2 weather warning is on record.
   - **Language:** no case records what language any warning was in. The relocated are invisible to the warning system everywhere, which ties this directly to our thesis.
7. **Who gets moved:** the poor, informal and tenure-insecure, often a marginalised group at place level.
   - South Africa: isiZulu-speaking townships and informal settlements.
   - India: Dalit-majority communities in Chennai.
   - Bangladesh: stateless Rohingya.
   - USA: mostly African American tenants.
   - Bangladesh chars: landless river-island dwellers.
8. **Cheap land is flood land:** marsh (Chennai), former rice fields (Philippines, Indonesia), river islands (Bangladesh), floodway (Houston) and riverbank (Durban). The poorest people are placed on land no one else wants.

## What is different about South Africa
1. **The warning came from inside government.** In most cases the warning came from outsiders: IOM, Human Rights Watch or activists. In Durban it came from the state's own Auditor-General, in an official report to Parliament. The state knew, on paper, through its own institutions.
2. **A double standard on blame.** Officials repeatedly told residents not to build near rivers, while the city itself put a camp on a riverbank. Only Indonesia (Kohod) shows a similar pattern of blaming residents.
3. **It was a disaster response, not a clearance.** In Chennai, Manila and Bogura, flood safety was the stated reason for evicting people onto worse land. In Durban, flood victims were moved into the next flood. The closest match is Haiti: a camp for displaced people, flagged in writing, called temporary, and never fixed.
4. **A democracy with oversight that produced nothing.** South Africa has an active auditor, a free press, NGOs (Amnesty) and competitive local elections, and still had no inquiry, like Haiti, Bangladesh and India. The only case that reached a court was in the USA. **Having oversight did not produce consequences.**
5. **Poor services add a second way to die.** The Isipingo deaths were electrocutions, and the Lamontville camp sat above a high-voltage cable. The same pattern appears in the flood deaths across our wider dataset: all 13 electrocution deaths were at sites where under 10% speak English.

## For the story
Lamontville is one example of a global pattern: **the state moves the displaced onto cheap flood land, calls it temporary, is warned, and when the flood comes, blames the rain or the residents.** South Africa is distinctive because its own auditor raised the warning, which shows the problem is not missing information but missing consequences. And in all 22 cases, no one checked whether a warning reached the people the state itself had put in harm's way.

## Caveats
- **Case finding:** cases were found by targeted search, so this is not a census, and failures are more likely to be reported than successes.
- **Blocked sources:** some figures rest on search-engine excerpts because pages were blocked (ReliefWeb, some OCHA and IOL pages). These are marked in the JSON, and their confidence is lowered.
- **Government's role:** in Great Love Village (an NGO build), Kohod (government or developer), Bentiu (a UN site) and Dikwa, the government's role in choosing the site is partial or unclear.
- **Amnesty wording:** the Amnesty "close to the riverbank" wording comes from our own extract of the report. The research agent could read only IOL's summary.
- **Death counts:** "nf" means none were found, not that there were zero deaths.
