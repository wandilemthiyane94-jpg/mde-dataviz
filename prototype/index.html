The watched sky

South Africa's weather service watches the country continuously, and a colour-coded warning system links its forecasts to disaster managers.

    Only facts marked verified in data/forecasting_infrastructure.json are shown here.

    The chain

    When a storm forms, the system sees it. A warning is issued in Pretoria and passed to provincial and municipal disaster centres.

    Lines are schematic: Pretoria to provincial capitals (approximate positions). Radar ring: only sites with published coordinates are drawn.

    The warning goes out

    The warning is light. It reaches everywhere.

    Here, a place glows only as brightly as the share of its residents who speak English, the language the warning is written in, as their first language.

    Brightness = Census 2011 English first-language share at each death site (data/death_sites_geocoded.json). Warning language: data/warning_language.json.

     

    What doesn't fit

    The bright places where people died are mostly roads, causeways and river crossings: motorists passing through, not residents.

    We show them because a pattern you can trust has to include its exceptions.

    Sites with English first-language share > 50% are highlighted.
