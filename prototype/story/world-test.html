World test · 64 random relocations · Hollie and Wandile

Who holds the decision

We drew 64 planned relocations at random from a global inventory of 408 and coded how each was run before looking at how it ended. Moving people is almost never owned by one agency. The outcome depends on whether the people being moved had a say.

3 · What we found

Ownership of the decision, not of the org chart

1 of 36

relocations with a known structure had one public body running it end to end. Almost nobody works that way, so "one owner" can't be tested.

88% · 87%

not a success, with or without a clear lead agency. A clear lead agency alone changes nothing.

43%

not a success when the community started or co-ran the move, against 92% when it didn't (p = 0.014). The same split holds when residents helped choose the land (p = 0.038).

17 of 18

top-down government relocations were judged mixed or failed.

33% · 100%

of new sites flooded or were exposed, with and without a binding hazard check (n = 15, p = 0.077).

55% · 50%

of new sites hit or exposed in richer and poorer countries. Money is not the difference.

South Africa's TRAs combine every bad-outcome feature. The municipality decides alone, residents don't choose the site, and no flood check binds it: camps sit on flood ground at the same rate as random land (39% vs 34%). And the sites are "temporary".

4 · How much we know

Most relocations are never followed up

  • Each test uses between 12 and 35 cases. We ran 30 main tests, and none clears a Bonferroni threshold (0.002). Read these results as a pattern to test further, not as proof.
  • Cases that were written about are not random. They are often community-led, or went badly.
  • One team coded every case from public sources. Every code carries a quote, a URL and a confidence rating so it can be re-coded.
  • The inventory is weighted to Asia, the Americas and the Pacific: Africa has 5 of the 64 cases.
