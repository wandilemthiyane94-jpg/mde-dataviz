Who owns the flood?

This is South Africa's chain from flood forecast to permanent home: 8 links, held by 3 spheres of government. Nobody owns it from end to end. The next 9 pages show how we found that out.

Chain reconstructed from the National Housing Code Vol. 4 (Emergency Housing Programme), eThekwini's briefing to Parliament (8 Mar 2023), the Auditor-General's flood-relief report (Aug 2022) and National Treasury disaster grants 2022/23. Details on page 8.

Before the reveal — The usual answers6 / 10
Is it that the state doesn't care? That people are poor?

Those make it worse. They don't explain it.

The state doesn't care

The state acted fast: an R516m first round of flood funding, 71 transit camps built, and an Auditor-General audit within 4 months.

Plenty of activity; the gap is elsewhere
ReliefWeb/Treasury 2022 · IOL 2021 · AGSA 2022

The people are poor

Vunidogoloa, a village of 26 households, moved to safety. Gramalote turned its 40% renters into owners. Poor communities were moved safely where someone owned the outcome.

Raises exposure; doesn't decide it
McMichael 2019 · Natural Hazards Center

Corruption

Found in 3 of the 4 failures where there was evidence to check. But Mozambique and Somalia failed without it.

Makes it worse; doesn't cause it
IOL R32m probe 2023 · AP (Haiti) · rootcause matrix

Incompetence

In every failure, someone had flagged the site first: the Auditor-General, a UN agency, a national audit, a study.

They knew
AGSA 2022 · IOM 2010 · CAG 2014 · 2004 study

Climate

Grantham flooded again in 2013. The families it had moved to the hilltop stayed dry.

The same rain falls on every case
Okada et al. 2014

Money

Haiti had large amounts of international money and still built on flood land. Fiji and Colombia got it right.

Doesn't separate the cases

State 07 — The reveal9 / 10
The problem wasn't that nobody knew.
The problem was that nobody owned the whole outcome.

The forecast sat with SAWS. The flood map sat with the city. The warning about the site sat with the Auditor-General. The shelters belonged to the province. The families sat on the riverbank.

South Africa has the information. The institutions that possess it do not control the decisions that determine exposure.

Sources: SAWS Durban S-band radar location: WMO Radar Database / OSCAR (data/observing_network.json) · eThekwini Flood Plain 100yr · Auditor-General, Aug 2022 ("some units… built on unsuitable land") · Mail & Guardian, 9 May 2022 (councillor ordered families back to the flood-prone camp) · IOL / Amnesty 2025 (Lamontville deaths) · Housing Code Vol. 4 (province owns the temporary units).

Method and sources10 / 10

Question. Why does South Africa move flood-displaced people onto land that floods again, and what do the countries that don't do this have in common?

Sites. "South Africa TRAs 2022–2026": 84 temporary relocation sites (TRAs, TEAs, TRUs and transit camps), each with its own sources. Transcribed to data/tra_sites_2022_2026.csv. Why each site was created is coded from the list's type and notes; 78 sites have coordinates, mostly estimates (±1–3 km).

Satellite water. JRC Global Surface Water occurrence 1984–2021, 30 m Landsat (Pekel et al. 2016). Tiles cached in data/raw/gsw_tiles; Durban mosaic recoloured by methods/scripts/recolor_durban_water.ps1 (occurrence under 50% = intermittent).

Floodplain test. eThekwini "Flood Plain 100yr" FeatureServer. Each Durban site is tested as inside, within 250 m, or within 1 km. Baseline: 300 seeded random points across Durban's urban area, on the land side of the coast (data/raw/durban_random_baseline.csv).

Reflooding. Counts only floods documented at the relocation site after people moved in: the site list's status notes, our case research, and a sweep of 30 of the 84 sites (data/raw/tra_reflood_sweep.json; high and medium confidence counted, low reported separately). If a site isn't counted, that means no flood was documented, not that it stayed dry.

World cases. 24 relocation-into-risk cases (data/relocation_cases.csv); in-depth reviews of 5 failures and 5 successes (data/raw/relocation_deep_*.json), scored on 10 factors (data/relocation_rootcause_matrix.csv).

Limits. The world cases were chosen because of how they turned out, so this is a pattern, not proof. Codes are our reading of sourced findings. Some quotes rest on search excerpts and are tagged in the JSON. Gramalote was a landslide, not a flood. The Beledweyne evidence is a submitted manuscript that may not yet be peer-reviewed.
