Data sources and method

  • Site list. “Temporary Relocation Areas (TRAs) in South Africa, compiled from 2022–2026 online sources” (compiled 27 Sep 2026). It covers TRAs, transit camps, temporary residential units (TRUs) and temporary emergency accommodation (TEA). Each row has one or two sources, a dated status note, the size as reported, and the status. Sources are mainly GroundUp, IOL, Daily Maverick, the Mail & Guardian, the Parliamentary Monitoring Group, and municipal and provincial records. File: data/raw/South_Africa_TRAs_2022-2026.xlsx.
  • Who was moved (our coding). One code per site (flood survivors, evicted, cleared for a project, Covid-19 de-densification, fire survivors, upgrading decant, or not stated), with its basis recorded. File: data/tra_sites_exposure.csv (column origin).
  • Coordinates. Estimated from named landmarks (±1–3 km) unless marked “Published”. Locations are approximate on purpose, and nothing identifies individual homes.
  • Floodplain test (eThekwini only). 28 Durban sites with coordinates were tested against eThekwini Municipality's Flood Plain 100yr open data: inside, within 250 m, within 1 km, or beyond. The same test on 300 seeded random points gives the baseline: 11 of 28 sites (39%) are inside or within 250 m, against 34% of random ground (binomial p = 0.34). Script: methods/scripts/build_tra_exposure.ps1.
  • Flooded since? A site-by-site news search of 30 of the 84 sites for flooding after people moved in. It found 7 documented cases plus 2 low-confidence ones. Each has its evidence and a confidence rating. A blank means not found or not yet searched, not “never flooded”. File: data/raw/tra_reflood_sweep.json.
  • What this data can't tell you. How many people live in each site today (sizes are as reported, in mixed units), who they are as individuals (we don't name residents), and whether moving them was justified.

Combined table: data/tra_sites_full.csv (rebuild with methods/scripts/build_tra_sources.ps1). Repository: github.com/wandilemthiyane94-jpg/mde-dataviz.
