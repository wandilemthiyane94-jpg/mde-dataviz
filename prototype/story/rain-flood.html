How it's made. A relative elevation model built with the IDW method from the REM tutorials Josh shared (Dan Coe; OpenTopography's RiverREM). The terrain is AWS Terrain Tiles at zoom 14 (8.3 m pixels, about 30 m source data here). River channels are traced from the terrain itself (sink filling, D8 flow accumulation, catchments of 0.2 km² or more), plus JRC Global Surface Water cells. The river surface is interpolated from those cells, and the model is the terrain minus that surface. Script: methods/scripts/build_rem.ps1.

What it is and isn't. The rising water is a "bathtub" fill: everything below the chosen height floods at once. Real floods have flow, timing and drainage, so this shows exposure, not a flood forecast. At this resolution the camp sits on the valley floor, on a traced drainage line. Our team's sharper cross-section puts it 2–4 m above the river. The coarse terrain blurs riverbanks and canal walls, so the pattern across the valley is reliable but single-site heights are not, until we use finer elevation data (city LiDAR).
