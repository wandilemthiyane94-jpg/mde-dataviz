What this does and doesn't show

Durban floods drain fast. The satellite passes over every 12 days. The first pass after the April 2022 peak came 5 days later, and by then only swollen river channels were left. For February 2025 the next pass came about 10 days after the flash flood, and no new water shows up. A missing red patch here doesn't mean a place stayed dry.

So the radar can't settle where the water went. The city's floodplain map, field reports and high-resolution images of damage (which we have requested from SANSA) have to do that. This is why the story leans on the floodplain map.

Method: Sentinel-1 RTC gamma0 VV, 10 m, same orbit (relative orbit 43) for each pair, cropped by the Microsoft Planetary Computer data API. 5×5 mean filter; water below −16 dB; new water means water after, not water before, and at least 3 dB darker. Sea and permanent water from JRC Global Surface Water (occurrence ≥ 50%) are masked out. Script: methods/scripts/build_s1_flood.ps1. The −16 dB threshold is a common default and hasn't been calibrated against field data here.
