Lamontville, Durban · 26 February 2025

A small storm. A camp the city built on the riverbank.

About 35 mm of rain fell in four hours. The riverside camp flooded; the camp beside it, five metres higher, stayed dry. Three children were swept away.

03 · What we borrow

Five references, one interface

Eviction Lab

A map you read as a sentence

"Show conditions at 12 temporary sites in 2026." The orange words are the controls. Their grey "No data" swatch becomes our honest grey.

Manhattan, hour by hour

Time as the animation

Their map breathes through a day. Ours plays 2007 to 2026: sites appear, close, and their "temporary" bars keep growing.

Manhattan in 3D bars

Height means something

In the Time tab, bar height is years spent "temporary". Gwala Street, since 2008, towers over the rest.

Anti-Eviction Mapping Project

Layers that lift apart

Flood plain, temporary sites, people and missing data are separate sheets of one map. Stack them, click one, and it tells its part of the story.

First Street

Open on the flood itself

The landing is the Gwala Street simulation, already raining and rising, drawn as an exploded block: rain, terrain, ground and water.

A chat-style search bar

Start from a name

Type a site, a family's route or a question. Version one matches our own data; a later version could answer in sentences, quoting sources.
