The night the water came

A simulation of the April 2022 storm over the uMlazi valley, Durban: real rainfall, real terrain, water running downhill hour by hour, ending at the Lamontville riverside camp.

Layers. Satellite photo: Sentinel-2, 29 March 2022, two weeks before the floods. Terrain and hillshade: AWS Terrain Tiles. Rain: ERA5 hourly reanalysis at the camp (Open-Meteo); reanalysis smooths out local downpours, so the real rain was heavier in places. Water: a simplified rain-on-grid model on 33 m cells (terrain pits filled, 70% runoff, Manning flow), plus river water entering from upstream: the uMlazi (905 km² catchment) and five smaller streams, traced from wider terrain and routed at 1.5 m/s. Scripts: build_upstream_inflow.ps1, build_flood_sim.ps1. Camp location: our site map.

What it is and isn't. It's a visual simulation built to show how rain becomes flooding, not an engineering flood model. It uses one rain gauge point for the whole catchment and doesn't represent canal walls, culverts or storm drains. The 33 m cells blur the canal bank, so the camp depth includes the channel next to it. Read depths as indicative. The camp did flood in April 2022 (Mail & Guardian, 9 May 2022), and again in February 2025, when three children were swept away (GroundUp).
